# Recall-relevance campaign — 2026-10-21 (F1, 16 GiB)

**Verdict: block-3 feasibility data, not a quality conclusion and not an
activation.** The scoring protocol is stable (224/224 candidates scored, 0
failures, 0 retries across both waves). At the frozen 8000-token equal
consultation budget, JEV and recency are complementary (1–2 of 4 annotated
items each); the controls (chronological, digest-shaped pointers) select 0/4
in every zone; the two **critical** annotations (both in `:thinking` blocks)
are missed by every method, including on the only zone with 100 % scored-pool
coverage. Prefix reuse works but is flaky at 100 % pool occupancy
(32 % / 32 % / 6 % of candidates reused the shared prefix in the no-anchor
wave; the anchored wave measured 100 % / ~87 % / 0 % per zone).

## Quality at equal budget (8000 tokens, same scored pool per zone)

Items covered per method (`critical` = 2 items, both `:thinking`):

| Zone (pool coverage) | jev | recency | chronological | digest pointers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1K (22 %) | 0/4 | **serializer** | 0/4 | 0/4 |
| 2K (46 %) | **explicit-go** | **explicit-go, serializer** | 0/4 | 0/4 |
| 4K (100 %) | **explicit-go** | **serializer** | 0/4 | 0/4 |

- The controls (chronological, digest-shaped pointers) select 0/4 in every
  zone — they discriminate, and they are useless for this case.
- JEV and recency are complementary, not a winner: JEV takes `explicit-go`
  (2K, 4K), recency takes `serializer` (1K, 4K); neither covers a critical
  item anywhere.
- On the 4K zone — the only one where all 4 items were inside the scored
  pool (100 % coverage) — `top20` and `template-safety` (the two critical,
  thinking-resident items) are still missed by every method at the 8000
  budget. Whether that is a budget shortfall, a JEV scoring miss on
  thinking-resident items, or a case artifact is not distinguishable at
  n = 1 compaction.

## Limitations

- One frozen compaction, 4-item annotation set, partial pools (1K 22 %, 2K
  46 %). Per the source contract: feasibility evidence, not
  generalization, and not a retrieval-quality claim.
- The method comparison is confounded by pool coverage on 1K/2K; the 4K
  zone (100 %) is the cleanest — and there it is a tie-with-misses.
- Cache flakiness at 100 % occupancy (16 GiB pool refilled by live-session
  image churn: every model request writes a 146.8 MiB endpoint image) makes
  the latency numbers occupancy-dependent; a 24 GiB pool or the F2 code
  lever would be needed for stable reuse (see DIAGNOSIS doc).
- Scores are informational rankings (JEV choice probabilities), not
  evidence of item correctness.

## What this means for block 4 (integration decision)

The prototype is stable end-to-end (224 candidate completions, 0 protocol
failures) and the evaluation harness discriminates (useful methods vs zero
controls). It has **not** demonstrated superiority over recency on a
critical item, and it has not demonstrated full-zone retrieval quality.
Options for the user: (a) a second frozen (held-out) compaction to test
generalization before any activation claim; (b) 24 GiB pool + full-pool
campaign for clean cache/latency numbers; (c) stop here — the prototype
stays feasible and inactive, and block 4 closes without integration.
